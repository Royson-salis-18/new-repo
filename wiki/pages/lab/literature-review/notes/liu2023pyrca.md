# PyRCA (Liu et al., Salesforce 2023)

> Notes from the **full text** (technical report, 12 pages). Page numbers are PDF pages.

## 1. Summary
An open-source Python library offering a unified interface for metric-based RCA: anomaly detectors, causal graph construction (PC, GES), scoring (random walk, hypothesis testing from CIRCA, epsilon-diagnosis, RCD, Bayesian inference), expert-knowledge constraints and a GUI dashboard. The only experiment is a benchmark on synthetic data generated by the library.

## 2. Problem and motivation
- Production systems have thousands of KPI metrics; manual checking is slow; no open one-stop RCA library existed (pp.1-3). Argued without data.

## 3. Method
- Two-phase models (graph then score) and one-phase models (RCD, epsilon-diagnosis) (pp.6-9). Expert knowledge YAML: root nodes, leaf nodes, forbidden and required edges (p.5).

## 4. Data and experimental setup (p.9)
- 500 random DAGs (20 nodes, 30 edges), 5000 samples each, root cause nodes and mechanism changes random; Recall@k averaged over graphs.

## 6. Limitations
- Stated: causal discovery needs assumptions (linear, no latent confounders); expert knowledge helps (p.9).
- **My critique:**
  1. The synthetic data follow the structural-causal-model assumptions of the hypothesis-testing method (the generator is described as inspired by the same group's CIRCA experiments), so its perfect Recall@1 shows the method works when its own assumptions hold, not on real systems.
  2. No real dataset, no microservice benchmark, no comparison to trivial baselines such as anomaly-score ranking.
  3. Technical report with no peer review.
- Not discussed: failure-time misspecification; non-linear or confounded data.
